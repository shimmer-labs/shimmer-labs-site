Text:

## The trap: solving typing with more software

Search for help with business paperwork and you'll find a wall of apps: field service platforms, invoicing suites, CRMs. Some are genuinely good. But they all share a cost nobody puts on the pricing page: your whole crew has to learn them, your history has to migrate into them, and your business now runs on their roadmap.

If the actual problem is "a human spends hours retyping things," you may not need new software at all. You need the retyping to stop.

## Automation that fits what you already run

The systems we build sit alongside your existing tools instead of replacing them:

- A gym owner kept her booking platform, which famously has no API for the things she needed. <a href="/case-studies/sweat-yoga-fitness">The AI operates it through the same screens she used</a>, following her rules, with her approval on anything customer-facing.
- A landscaper kept his customer database. <a href="/case-studies/stillwater-landscaper">The photo-reading system writes into it</a> exactly like the person at the keyboard did.
- Shops on QuickBooks stay on QuickBooks. The AI does the entry; a human approves before anything posts.

Your crew changes nothing about how they work. That matters more than any feature list, because the tool your team actually uses beats the better tool they resist.

## When new software IS the right answer

Sometimes the honest answer is an off-the-shelf app. If you're starting from zero with no system at all, a purpose-built tool like QuickBooks or a field service app is the right first move, and we'll say so. Automation shines when you already have systems that mostly work and a human bridging the gaps between them by hand.
