## The clipboard test

The best test we know is a real one. A Stillwater landscaping crew ends every day with a route sheet: handwritten, different crew members' handwriting, part English, part Spanish, notes squeezed into the margins. For years, somebody typed that into the customer database every night. About two hours, at the end of a ten-hour day.

Now the owner takes one photo. The AI reads all of it. <a href="/case-studies/stillwater-landscaper">Here's the full story with the actual route sheet</a>.

## Reading is the easy part. Understanding is the point.

Old-school scanning software (OCR) turns pen strokes into letters, and that's where it stops. Modern AI language models do something more useful: they understand what the words mean in context.

On that route sheet, "cut the hedges, pulled weeds" isn't just text. It's an upsell that needs to go on an invoice. "Sprinklers were on, skipped it" isn't just a note. It's a yard that needs rescheduling. The AI makes those calls the way the person at the keyboard used to, and anything it can't read with confidence gets flagged for a human instead of guessed at.

## What this means for your paperwork

If your business runs on paper that a human currently retypes, that job is probably automatable:

- Handwritten job sheets and work orders
- Vendor invoices, printed or PDF
- Receipts from the supply house
- Notes from a site walkaround that become an estimate

The rule we use: if the task is reading something, understanding it, and writing it down somewhere else, a computer does it better now. The judgment calls stay with you. The typing doesn't have to.
