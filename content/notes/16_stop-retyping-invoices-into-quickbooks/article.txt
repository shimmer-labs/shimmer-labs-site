## Why this eats so much time

We timed it with a plumbing and HVAC shop near Stillwater. One vendor invoice took about an hour to get from a PDF into QuickBooks. Open the PDF, find the invoice number, retype every line, pick a category, check the total, attach the file. Their office guy spent about 40 hours a month on it. At his wage, that's roughly $1,000 a month of labor spent typing.

The wage is not the real cost. When we asked what he'd do with those 40 hours, he said "send estimates." Every hour on invoices was an hour a quote did not go out. Some of those quotes would have closed. That's the number that should bother you, and it never shows up on a report.

If you want the money math, including when automating is not worth it, read [what it costs to automate invoicing](/notes/cost-to-automate-invoicing). This note is about the how.

## Way one: what QuickBooks can already do, and where it stops

QuickBooks Online has receipt and bill capture built in, and plenty of owners have never opened it. We checked Intuit's own help pages before writing this. Here is what it does as of this writing.

There are four ways in. Upload from your computer. Pull from Google Drive. Forward to a custom email address that ends in @assist.intuit.com. Or snap a photo in the QuickBooks mobile app. QuickBooks reads the file and pulls the date, amount, vendor, and last four digits of the card. It creates a transaction for you to review in the Receipts tab. From there you can edit it, add it to an account, or match it to a bank transaction that already came through.

A few rules from the help pages. Files must be PDF, JPEG, JPG, GIF, or PNG. One receipt per file. Email attachments have to be between 46KB and 20MB, and the sender must be a QuickBooks user with permission. Intuit says the feature is limited on the QuickBooks Online Free and Lite plans, so check your plan before you count on it.

For a gas receipt or a one-page supply house ticket, this is a good tool and you should use it. Where it stops:

- **Multi-page supplier PDFs.** The help pages describe pulling a date, an amount, and a vendor. A 40-line distributor invoice needs the lines, or you never know what you paid for.
- **Handwritten tickets.** A counter ticket with a scrawled part number is a different problem. The good news is [AI reads handwriting now](/notes/can-ai-read-handwriting), and Spanish too.
- **Coding to jobs.** Nothing on the invoice says which job the copper went to. No capture tool can fill that in, so someone still makes that call one invoice at a time.

## Way two: a prompt you can run tonight

For the messy PDFs, use an AI assistant directly. Most of them accept an uploaded PDF or a phone photo. Paste this in, then attach the invoice:

> You are helping a small business bookkeeper enter a vendor invoice into QuickBooks. I will paste the invoice text or attach a photo or PDF. Read it and return:
>
> 1. A table with: Vendor, Invoice number, Invoice date, Due date, Subtotal, Tax, Total.
> 2. A second table with one row per line item: Description, Quantity, Unit price, Line total, Suggested expense category.
> 3. One line saying whether the line totals add up to the invoice total. If they do not, say so.
> 4. A list called "Check these": anything unreadable, anything you guessed, and anything odd, like a duplicate line or freight with no items.
>
> Do not invent values. If a field is missing or unreadable, write "unclear" and put it in the "Check these" list. Use these expense categories when they fit: [paste your QuickBooks category list here].

How to use it: run one real invoice and compare the output to the PDF line by line. Where it missed, fix the prompt. Add your actual QuickBooks category list. Once it's right for one supplier, it's usually right for that supplier from then on. Now you type from a clean table instead of squinting at a PDF, and most of the hour is gone.

Two rules. Never paste anything with a full card number or bank details into a chat window. And the rule we state on every page: a human approves before anything posts to the books. The AI reads and drafts. You post.

That is the do-it-yourself rung. If you'd rather do it with someone next to you, bring three invoices to [office hours](/office-hours) and we'll tune the prompt together for free.

## What not to automate

The phone call when a bill is wrong. If a supplier charged you for 12 fittings and shipped 10, that's a conversation between people. The AI's job is to flag the mismatch, not make the call. Same with the decision to pay. Which bills go out this Friday and which wait is a cash decision, and it stays with the owner. We build the system to put a clean, coded bill in front of you. What you do with it is still your job, and it should be.
