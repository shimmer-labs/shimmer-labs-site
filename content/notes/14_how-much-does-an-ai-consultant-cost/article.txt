## The three ways to pay an AI consultant, and what each costs

Every AI consulting quote you get will be one of three shapes. Hourly, project, or retainer. What you'll see quoted online is $150 to $300 an hour, $5,000 to $25,000 for a fixed-scope project, and retainers from $500 a month. Those numbers are averaged across the whole country, and they hide more than they show.

**Hourly.** You pay for time, and the bill shows up after the work. Hourly punishes a consultant for being fast and rewards them for being slow. It also means you can't know what a fix costs until it's done. We don't bill hourly for exactly that reason. If we fix your scheduling mess in twenty minutes, you should get the fix, not a tiny invoice and a shrug.

**Project.** You pay a fixed price for a defined piece of work. This is fine when the scope is real. The problem with the $5,000 to $25,000 range is that it's wide enough to mean anything. A project quote is only useful when it names the process, the deliverable, and who owns the result. Our version of a project is the <a href="/services/assessment">Operations Assessment</a>, and it costs $1,500 or $2,500 depending on headcount.

**Retainer.** You pay a flat monthly fee for ongoing help. The $500 a month figure you'll see online usually buys a monthly check-in call and not much else. At the other end, AI automation agencies quote retainers from $5,000 a month, and most of them never set foot in your building. Our retainer is the <a href="/services/concierge">AI Concierge</a>. It runs $1,000 to $3,000 a month depending on team size, with no contract.

## How to know if it paid for itself

The only honest test is hours. A consultant pays off when the hours saved cost more than the fee. So you need to know what an hour costs in your building. We use about $33 an hour fully loaded in Stillwater and about $40 in Oklahoma City and Tulsa. That's the BLS county wage times 1.3 for payroll tax, insurance, and overhead. We showed the full table in <a href="/notes/ai-consultant-cost-per-employee">what an AI consultant costs per employee</a>.

Take a Crew client. Eight people in Stillwater, $1,500 a month.

- $1,500 divided by $33 an hour is about 45 hours a month to break even.
- 45 hours across eight people is about 5.7 hours per person per month.
- That's about 1.3 hours per person per week.

The Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis surveyed workers in 2024 and found the ones who actually use generative AI save about 2.2 hours a week. At that rate, eight people get back about 76 hours a month, worth about $2,500 at $33 an hour. That's a $1,000 monthly surplus before you count the owner's hours, which are worth a lot more than $33. We broke down the hours-per-employee numbers in <a href="/notes/hours-ai-saves-per-employee">how many hours AI saves per employee</a>.

Run the same math for your team. If the break-even hours look easy to hit, the price is fine. If they don't, don't buy it, from us or anybody.

## Red flags in an AI consulting quote

We've seen a lot of quotes come through office hours. These are the ones that should make you close the PDF.

- **No written scope.** If the proposal doesn't say what gets built or fixed, you're buying time, not results.
- **Hourly with no cap.** You are funding an open tab. Ask for a ceiling or walk.
- **"AI transformation" with no named process.** If they can't say which task gets faster, they don't know yet. Transformation is a word people use when they don't have a plan.
- **You don't own what gets built.** If the automations live in their accounts, you're renting your own operations. Every account, every workflow, every login should be yours.
- **No one comes to the building.** Processes live in the building. Someone has to watch how the work actually happens, not how the owner describes it on a Zoom call.
