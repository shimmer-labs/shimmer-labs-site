Text:

## The numbers

- **Sales tax collections are up 10% year over year** since the fiscal year started July 1, and use tax is up 31%. On the chart the city showed, Stillwater's sales tax growth since mid 2020 is outpacing Enid, Moore, Midwest City, and Owasso.
- **The Google data center** has already generated more than $1.5 million in new sales and use tax from construction spending since late 2024. Over the next couple of decades the mayor put the city's take at more than $100 million, with 90% earmarked for public infrastructure and maintenance. Nearly 2,000 workers are on the construction project right now.
- **1,200 new housing units** are in the application process at Development Services, with hundreds more approved and waiting on construction.
- **The new airport terminal is open.** 31,000 square feet, three times the old one, two gates, a jet bridge, opened in August alongside the tenth anniversary of American Airlines service.
- **The new high school** is 188,000 square feet with a culinary lab, a commercial kitchen, fabrication space, and a gym that doubles as a storm shelter.
- **Finance got its 44th consecutive** certificate for excellence in financial reporting. Fewer than 5% of governmental units in the country get it.
- **Dispatch was named Oklahoma 911 Center of the Year**, partly for the response during the March 2025 wildfires.

## What's being built

- **Manufacturing.** Kingspan is building an advanced manufacturing facility on formerly vacant property, more than 100 jobs, and part of its supply chain is co-locating with a planned $100 million facility on the same property. USA Rare Earth is operating a rare earth magnet plant in the building that used to print ESPN The Magazine and Rolling Stone, one of a handful producing those magnets anywhere in the Western Hemisphere.
- **Meridian Technology Center** is the training provider for the data center project and has bought land in Commerce Park for a 12,000 square foot training facility: forklift operation, industrial maintenance, welding, cable splicing.
- **The YMCA.** 56,000 square feet, two pools, an indoor walking track, a culinary facility. Officially winter 2027 to 2028. The Y's director told the room he expects it about a year out.
- **Strickland Park** is getting a $1.8 million makeover funded by the visitor sales tax: turf infields, LED lighting, real restrooms. Fewer rained-out tournaments, more visiting teams spending money here.
- **Water.** A new finished water pump station rated for 24 million gallons a day, expandable to 30, finishing commissioning by the end of the year. A seven mile, 36 inch pipeline from Lake McMurtry to the treatment plant. Tank rehabs at Manning, Washington, and Stallard, and the Yost tower is up for replacement.
- **Streets.** The $10 million pavement plan finished Monticello Drive, the intersection at Monticello and University, and downtown Main Street intersections with four-way stops and LED signs. Next year: Knoblock, 4th, and Lincoln. Highway 51 reconstruction is back on ODOT's schedule for fall 2029.
- **Downtown.** The old library at 6th and Husband becomes the Bookend Hotel. New restaurants Nixta and Chuck's. A Barnes and Noble. Block 34 has been open a year and is booked with markets, movies, and concerts. The convention center planned for Duck Street is not moving forward; bids have been coming back about 30% over estimate.
- **Rails to trails** switched engineering firms mid-planning. The state's money is in the bank and construction should start by the end of next year.

## What's changing at City Hall

- A **new outage management system** launches at the end of the month: report electric and water outages by text, get updates on your phone, see a live outage map.
- A **full rewrite of the land development code**, which is nearly 20 years old. The stated goals are more housing options and new investment. If you build, develop, or lease, this is the one to watch.
- A **new enterprise resource planning system** for finance, HR, and utilities. The mayor was candid that projects like this are hard.
- **Wayfinding and signage** across town with Visit Stillwater, and a new site, playstillwater.com, that starts with the activity instead of the department.
- **Design work has started** on a municipal tennis facility, an outdoor aquatics facility, and a parks vision plan. A new animal welfare facility breaks ground soon, opening winter 2027 to 2028. A fifth fire station is "getting close."

## The part nobody wanted to hear

Now that he can't run again, the mayor said the quiet part: almost all of the city's usable revenue is sales tax, which is volatile and which nobody at City Hall controls. Oklahoma City, Tulsa, and their suburbs fund long-term infrastructure with property tax bonds. Stillwater mostly doesn't. He'd like to see bonding capacity closer to 10 mills, starting with a bond for a pool or a fire station in the next few years, so that the transportation penny (about $15 million a year today) can eventually fund the things people actually ask for. Expect that conversation to keep coming up.

## What it means if you run a business here

- **Two thousand construction workers and 1,200 new housing units** are customers who don't know you yet. The mayor's own line about retail applies to every trade: every dollar spent in Edmond or Tulsa is a vote for Edmond or Tulsa getting the next store. The same is true of who they hire.
- **Sales tax up 10% means the room is busier**, and the paperwork gets busier with it. That's the season to fix the office side before it eats the evenings.
- **The Y, Strickland, the terminal, and the land code rewrite** are all demand signals for contractors, landscapers, cleaners, and anyone who serves the businesses that serve them.
- **Velvet Fudge got a shout-out from the podium** as a brand new business that became the center of a loud local music scene. Small and new counts here. Show up.
