Text:

## The name means two different things

**Meaning one: a chatbot.** Hotels and online stores have used "AI concierge" for years to mean a digital assistant that answers guest questions or recommends products. Software. You've chatted with one. This is the meaning most of the internet still uses.

**Meaning two: a person, on retainer, who does AI with you.** This is the new meaning, and it's the one spreading through the small business world right now. An AI concierge in this sense is a monthly service where someone who works with AI every day sits down with you, regularly, and automates your business alongside you. Not a chatbot. Not a course. Not an agency that disappears into a black box and hands you an invoice.

This article is about meaning two, because that's the one worth understanding before someone sells it to you.

## What the service actually includes

The shape is pretty consistent wherever you find it:

- **Standing working sessions.** Usually two a month. You share your screen, bring a real task that eats your week, and fix it together. You're at the keyboard.
- **Direct access between sessions.** Text or message the consultant when you're stuck mid-week, instead of saving up problems for a monthly meeting.
- **A shared record.** A running log of every session, everything built, and what's next, so you can judge at any moment whether the service is earning its keep.
- **A process-first approach.** The good ones fix the process before automating it. Automating a broken process just makes the mess faster.

The defining feature is the word WITH. You understand and own every automation you build, because you built it. If the relationship ends, the automations keep working and they're yours.

## Why it's suddenly everywhere

Small business owners spent two years hearing they should "use AI," tried a chatbot for a weekend, and stalled. Courses didn't fix it, because nobody finishes courses. Done-for-you agencies didn't fix it either, because owners ended up with automations they didn't understand, couldn't change, and lost the day they stopped paying.

Done-with-you closes that gap, and consultants have noticed. There are now playbooks circulating that teach consultants how to launch this exact offer. That's not a bad thing, but it does mean the quality is about to get uneven. Which brings us to the important part.

## Five questions to ask before you pay anyone for this

Ask these of any AI concierge, including us. The answers separate the real thing from a subscription trap.

1. **Whose accounts do the automations live in?** Correct answer: yours. If everything runs through the consultant's accounts, you're renting your own business processes.
2. **What happens when I cancel?** Correct answer: everything keeps working, and you understand it well enough to maintain it. If cancellation means losing your automations, that's a hostage situation with a monthly fee.
3. **Will I be at the keyboard?** Correct answer: yes, most of the time. If they do everything while you watch, you're buying an agency with extra meetings.
4. **What happens between sessions?** Correct answer: you can reach a human directly when you're stuck. Two calls a month with silence in between is a coaching program, not a concierge.
5. **Can I see a record of what my money produced?** Correct answer: yes, a shared log of every session and everything built. If they can't show receipts, the value gets fuzzy fast.
