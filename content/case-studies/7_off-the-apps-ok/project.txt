Description:

## The problem

Most event apps want to own where you find events. You can only pull from their feed, only share to their network, only RSVP through their wall. That works for them. It does not work for someone who just wants to actually show up to the thing on the flyer at the coffee shop.

People miss events because the path from "I saw a flyer" or "I saw an Instagram story" to "it's on my calendar" is too long.

## The arc

OffTheAppsOK started as a Stillwater event-discovery channel — flyers and Instagram stories curated into weekly roundups for locals trying to actually leave the house. Running that channel turned into a real engineering problem: parsing details out of blurry photos of flyers and screenshots of Instagram stories with consistent enough accuracy that the curation was trustworthy.

Prototyped the parsing pipeline in n8n first to prove it could hit **98% event accuracy** on the worst-case inputs (low-light flyer photos, Instagram screenshots cropped wrong, handwritten chalkboards). Once the accuracy held up, the pipeline became the engine for a native iOS app.

## What was shipped

- **Stillwater event curation channel** — weekly roundups published to Instagram, sourced from real flyers, screenshots, and submissions.
- **n8n parsing prototype** — Telegram intake → OpenAI Vision OCR → Google Sheets dedup → AI-written captions → Canva graphics → Instagram publish. Hit 98% event extraction accuracy on blurry, real-world inputs.
- **Native iOS app** — Swift / SwiftUI built around the same parsing engine. Shipped to the App Store in 2 months. Snap a flyer, snap a screenshot, get the event on your calendar.

## The point

The anti-lock-in framing matters: events should live where you can act on them, which means your calendar. Not someone else's feed. The iOS app proves the curation engine works wherever the source is.
