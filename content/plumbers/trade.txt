## We did the math with a local shop

We sat with a Stillwater-area plumbing and HVAC shop for a morning and just watched the office work happen. Their guy was spending about 40 hours a month getting vendor invoices out of PDFs and into QuickBooks. One invoice took an hour. At $24 an hour, that's roughly $1,000 a month of skilled labor spent typing.

And here's the part that stung: when we asked what he'd be doing instead, the answer was "sending estimates and talking to customers." The typing wasn't just costing wages. It was costing the jobs he never got to quote.

## What we automate for plumbing and HVAC shops

- **Vendor invoices into QuickBooks.** The invoice arrives by email or gets snapped with a phone. The AI reads it, codes it, and enters it. A human approves before anything posts.
- **Estimates.** Job notes and photos turned into a clean, priced estimate in your format, ready to send the same day.
- **Job paperwork.** Work orders, service records, and warranty info filed against the right customer without anyone retyping.
- **Receipts.** Parts runs to the supply house, snapped at the register, sorted straight into the bookkeeper's hands.

Each supplier or workflow is one build. Once the first one is running, adding another supplier is usually half the work, because the plumbing is already in place. Yes, we said plumbing.

## The proof

We've shipped this pattern before. <a href="/case-studies/stillwater-landscaper">A Stillwater landscaper's</a> two hours of nightly typing became one photo. <a href="/case-studies/sweat-yoga-fitness">A local gym owner</a> got her evenings back from membership paperwork. Different trades, same shape: read, understand, type. Computers do that part better now.
