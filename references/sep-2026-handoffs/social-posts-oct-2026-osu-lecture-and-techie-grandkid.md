## POST 1: the lecture

### LinkedIn

Thursday I stood in front of 110 freshmen in Legacy Hall and told them I built three apps nobody paid for.

Dr. Victoria Antin Yates teaches Intro to Entrepreneurship at Spears School of Business. She asked me to come talk about what starting a business actually looked like, not the textbook version. One unit in, these students already know the chapter: spot the opportunity, pick the idea, write the plan, launch. My year went: had a skill, started working, got told no a lot, rebuilt around whoever actually paid.

The part I most wanted them to hear was the contrast between my two businesses.

Shimmer Labs took 261 edits to the website before the message landed. Months of it. I'd explain what I did at a bar and watch eyes glaze about 20% of the way in.

Techie Grandkid I never pitched to anyone. I volunteer at a thrift store where the average volunteer is 70, and one of them asked if I could look at her TV. Then her phone. Then she asked if she could give my number to a friend. One flyer, zero ads, and people are paying. Same bar, same me, that one lands 9 times out of 10.

So the thing I'd tell freshman me: the customer is usually already standing next to you. Go talk to a person and watch their face. Don't ask the chatbot if your idea is good. It will say yes.

First time at the front of a lecture hall at my alma mater. I graduated from this place in 2009 and the room still felt big. Thank you Dr. Yates, Oklahoma State University, and the students who stayed after to ask the real questions.

Slides are at shimmerlabs.co/intro-entrepreneurship if you want them.

## POST 2: Techie Grandkid and the scam talk

### LinkedIn

Last year Americans reported $20.9 billion lost to online fraud. That's the FBI's number. It works out to about $40,000 a minute, and it only counts the people who reported it.

The part that got me is the age chart. The FTC tracks the median loss per victim by age. At 18 to 19 it's $250. At 80 and over it's $1,650. The older you are, the more they take, because the scripts are written for you: a grandkid in jail who needs bail money tonight, a bank fraud department that needs you to move your savings somewhere "safe," a Medicare card that has to be reissued before Friday.

I started Techie Grandkid because people in their 70s and 80s kept asking me for help with their phones, and every third conversation turned into "does this text look real to you?"

So I built a talk. It's called Don't Get Robbed Online. Thirty minutes, no jargon, three scams shown on a real phone screen so people can pattern-match them, and one family habit that beats all of them: a word only your family knows, that any caller asking for money has to say first.

I'm giving it at a Stillwater senior living community on the 16th. I'd like to give it in more rooms. Church groups, Rotary, the building your parents live in, a lunch for the staff who answer the phones at a bank. It's free. The people I'm trying to reach don't find things on the internet, so I need the people who do to bring me to them.

techiegrandkid.com, or message me here.
