# Operations Assessment: the deliverable

Two documents. The Snapshot is free and one page. The Assessment is paid, 10 to 15 pages, delivered as a shared digital copy plus a printed copy you walk through together. Fill the brackets. Delete any section that doesn't apply, but say why in the walkthrough.

House rules for both: plain words, no jargon, no em dashes, every number has a source (their words, your count, or a stated assumption), and AI is named as a tool where it fits, never as the headline. If the honest answer for a task is "you don't need AI for this," write that sentence.

## Part 2. The Operations Assessment (paid, 10 to 15 pages)

Timeline: intake form, then a day on site, then about two weeks to write, then the walkthrough. Printed copy is spiral-bound or stapled, single-sided so they can write on the backs. Digital copy is a shared doc they can comment on.

### Cover
**[Business name]. Operations Assessment.**
Prepared for [owner], [date]. Prepared by Logan Shimmer, Shimmer Labs, Stillwater, Oklahoma. Based on the intake form ([date]), a day on site ([date]), and conversations with [names and roles].

### Page 1. The one-page version
The whole document on one page, for the person who won't read the rest.
- Where you are: [two sentences]
- What it's costing: [N hours a week, $Y a month, one number]
- Fix first: [task, rung, hours back]
- Then: [task 2, task 3]
- Leave alone: [the one thing you're recommending they NOT automate]
- What it takes: [DIY: time. Concierge: tier and price. Sidecar: build estimate.]

### 1. Where you are
The business in one paragraph, then the team (name, role, what they actually touch on a computer or phone), then the tool inventory (every system, who owns the login, what it's for, what it costs a month). Then the five tasks from the Snapshot, now with real hours from watching and asking, not estimates.

### 2. How the work actually flows
Three process maps (five for 11 to 50 people). Pick the recurring tasks with the most hours or the most handoffs. For each:

**Process: [name]**
- Trigger: what starts it (a call, an email, a job closing, the first of the month)
- Steps: numbered, one line each, with who does it and which tool
- Handoffs: every place it moves from one person to another, and how (text, sticky note, "I just tell her")
- Where it breaks: the step that gets skipped, delayed, or redone. Their words.
- What's really going on: the paragraph where you say what you saw. Sometimes the handoff is the whole point, and the fix is a better handoff, not a computer.
- The cleaned-up version: same process with the unnecessary steps cut. Most 15-step processes need nine. Show the nine.

Draw the maps on the whiteboard on site, photograph them, and redraw them cleanly for the document. Put the whiteboard photo in the appendix.

### 3. The hours ledger
One table, every recurring task, no exceptions.

| Task | Who | How often | Hours per week | Loaded $/hr | $/month | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Source is "observed," "reported by [name]," or "assumed." Loaded rate: their payroll number times 1.3, or the county average from the BLS if they don't want to share ($33 Stillwater, $40 OKC and Tulsa as of late 2025). One number at the bottom. That number goes on page 1.

### 4. What to fix, in what order
One block per project, in the order you'd do them. Order by: fastest win first if they need to believe it works, biggest hours first if they already do.

**Project [N]: [name]**
- What changes: [before and after, two sentences]
- Which rung: Do it yourself / Build it together (Concierge) / Have it built and run (Sidecar)
- Tools: [what's involved, what's new, what it costs]
- Is AI part of this? [Yes: the specific step where a computer that reads and writes helps. / No: this is a process fix.]
- Hours back: [per week, and for whom]
- What could go wrong: [the exception that will break it, and what the human does when it does]
- If DIY: the prompt or the checklist, verbatim

### 5. The first 90 days
- Weeks 1 to 2: quick wins. [The two things that need no build. Prompts, a template, a rule.]
- Month 1: [the first real build, who's in the room, what "done" looks like]
- Month 2: [second build, or rollout of the first to the rest of the team]
- Month 3: [third, and the review: which hours actually came back]

### 6. What not to automate
The handoffs that are really conversations. The step where the owner's judgment is the product. The thing the customer notices. Name them, say why, and say what you'd do instead (a better handoff, a shared checklist, nothing).

### 7. What it costs, three ways
| Path | What you do | What it costs | Break-even |
|---|---|---|---|
| Do it yourself | [the prompts and templates in this document, your time] | Your hours: [N] to set up | Immediate if you actually do it |
| AI Concierge | Two sessions a month, we build together | [Tier] $[X]/mo, less $[credit] on month one | [N] hours a month across the team |
| Sidecar | We build and run the biggest piece | From $1,000 build plus $250/mo | [hours for that one process] |

Plenty of businesses do some of each. Say which combination you'd pick for them and why.

### 8. Next step
One paragraph. What you'd do Monday if it were your business. Then: the intake form is already on file, so if they want the Concierge, the first session can be booked from the walkthrough.

### Appendix
- Whiteboard photos from the on-site day
- Tool and login inventory (who owns what)

- Glossary, if any term in the document isn't plain English

## Walkthrough script (30 to 45 minutes, in person)
1. Hand over the printed copy. Page 1 first. Read it together. Stop for questions.
2. Process maps. Ask "is this how it actually happens?" for each. Mark corrections on their copy in pen.
3. The hours ledger. Ask which number surprised them.
4. The fix list. Ask which one they'd pick first, before telling them yours.
5. What not to automate. This is usually where they relax.
6. Costs, three ways. No pitch. "Here's what each path takes."
7. Leave the printed copy. Send the digital copy the same afternoon with the pen corrections applied.
